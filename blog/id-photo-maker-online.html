3. Background Color Requirements

The background color is one of the most common reasons ID photos get rejected. Here's a quick guide:

White background

  • US passport / visa
  • China passport / visa
  • India passport / visa / OCI
  • Schengen visa (most countries)
  • UK biometric residence permit

Light gray / off-white background

  • UK passport
  • EU / Schengen passport (varies)
  • Canada passport
  • Australia passport

Blue background

  • China one-inch and two-inch ID photos (work ID, school ID, graduation photos)
  • Some Chinese driving licences

Red background

  • China insurance cards, some Chinese ID contexts
  • Some visa photos for specific countries — always check official guidance

10. Step-by-Step Shooting Guide

A great source photo is 80% of the work. Here's how to shoot one at home:

Step 1: Set up the background

  • Plain white wall works for most documents
  • If you don't have a white wall, tape a white bedsheet or poster board flat against the wall
  • Avoid textured walls — they create shadows

Step 2: Lighting

  • Stand 1-2 meters from a window facing the wall (so light falls on your face, not behind you)
  • Use soft, diffuse daylight — early morning or late afternoon is ideal
  • Avoid direct sunlight — it creates harsh shadows
  • Avoid overhead lights alone — they cast shadows under the nose and chin
  • Use a white foam-core board on the opposite side as a reflector to fill shadows

Step 3: Position the subject

  • Stand or sit 1.5 meters from the wall (avoids hard background shadow)
  • Face the camera directly — head straight, shoulders level
  • Look at the camera, eyes open and visible
  • Neutral expression, mouth closed, no smile
  • Remove glasses unless your country explicitly allows them
  • Remove hats, head coverings (except religious — allowed if face is visible)
  • Push hair back from the face — forehead and eyebrows visible

Step 4: Camera setup

  • Use a phone with at least 8 MP camera
  • Hold the phone vertically at eye level of the subject (don't shoot up or down)
  • Frame so the head and top of shoulders fill the upper 70-80% of the frame
  • Use the rear camera (not selfie camera) for higher resolution
  • Have someone else shoot, or use a tripod and timer

Step 5: Take 5-10 photos

  • Review each shot — eyes open, neutral expression, even lighting
  • Delete the rejects immediately
  • Pick the best one — both eyes clearly visible, no shadow on face, hair off forehead

11. 7 Pro Tips for Compliant Photos

1. Read the official guidance first

Before shooting, check the official government source for your document. Sizes, background rules, and expression rules change. The US State Department, UK HMPO, and Schengen visa sites all publish their specs online.

2. Don't wear white on white

If your background is white, avoid a white shirt — the subject will blend in. Wear a darker colored top so the shoulders stand out. Reverse for dark backgrounds.

3. Keep the face uncovered

Hair must not cover the forehead, eyebrows, or eyes. Push it back with a clip or headband. Religious head coverings are allowed if the full face remains visible.

4. Avoid glare and reflections

Remove glasses (most countries don't allow them anyway). If your country allows glasses, use anti-reflective lenses and tilt the head slightly down to avoid flash glare.

5. Print on photo paper, not printer paper

Government offices reject photos printed on plain paper. Use glossy or matte photo paper at a drugstore kiosk, online print service, or home photo printer. The image-to-PDF tool can package your photos for printing.

6. Keep a digital copy

Save the high-resolution JPG/PNG in cloud storage. Many visa applications ask you to upload a digital copy in addition to prints. Compress a copy with imagec.xin/compress if the portal has a file size limit.

7. Use the print sheet for cheap copies

A 4×6 print sheet holds six 2×2 inch passport photos. Print one sheet at any drugstore photo kiosk for US$0.25-0.35 — instead of US$15 for two official photos. Cut them apart with scissors or a paper trimmer.

12. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I take a passport photo with my phone at home?
A: Yes. Use a phone with at least an 8 MP camera, stand 1.5 meters from a plain white wall in daylight, have someone photograph you at eye level, then upload to imagec.xin/idphoto to crop, set background, and meet the required size. The result prints at drugstore photo quality.

Q: What background color is required for a passport photo?
A: US passport requires plain white or off-white. China passport/visa uses white; China domestic ID often uses blue or red. Schengen/EU requires light gray or off-white. UK requires light grey or cream. Use imagec.xin/idphoto to swap backgrounds if your wall isn't compliant.

Q: Can I wear glasses in a passport photo?
A: As of 2026, the US, UK, Canada, and most EU countries prohibit glasses in passport photos, even prescription ones, due to glare and reflection issues. China allows non-tinted prescription glasses without frames covering eyes. The safest rule: remove glasses for any passport photo unless your country explicitly allows them.

Q: Can I smile in a passport photo?
A: Most countries require a neutral expression with mouth closed. The US, UK, Schengen, Canada, and Australia all require neutral expression. A slight, closed-mouth smile is sometimes tolerated, but any open-mouth smile or visible teeth will get your photo rejected. China requires a neutral expression with mouth closed.

Q: How much does it cost to make an ID photo online?
A: imagec.xin/idphoto is completely free — no sign-up, no watermark, runs in your browser. Drugstores (CVS, Walgreens) charge US$15-20 per pair of US passport photos. Post offices charge US$15. Passport photo apps charge US$5-10 per download. Making photos at home and printing at a 4×6 print service costs around US$0.25 per print.

Q: What size is a US passport photo?
A: A US passport photo is 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) square. The head (chin to crown) must be 1 to 1-3/8 inches (25-35 mm), which is 50-69% of the photo height. The eyes must sit 1-1/8 to 1-3/8 inches (28-35 mm) from the bottom. imagec.xin/idphoto auto-fits your photo to these exact specs.

Q: How do I print my ID photo after creating it?
A: Use the print sheet mode in imagec.xin/idphoto to generate a 4×6 layout with 6 photos. Save as JPG or PNG and print at any drugstore photo kiosk, online print service (Shutterfly, Snapfish), or home photo printer. Cut the photos apart with a paper trimmer. Cost is typically US$0.25-0.35 per sheet.
